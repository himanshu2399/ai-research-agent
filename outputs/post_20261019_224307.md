### Topic
Operating an internal LLM proxy to regain observability, policy, and cost control

### Research Summary
- A widely shared post argues that teams calling model APIs directly lack basic visibility and control across usage, policy, and cost, and advocates running an internal LLM proxy/gateway to restore observability and governance.
- The headroomlabs-ai/headroom project showcases a proxy pattern that compresses structured tool outputs/logs/JSON before they reach the LLM, claiming token reductions. This highlights a practical control point for cross-cutting optimizations.
- Key insight: a team-owned LLM proxy centralizes telemetry, enforces guardrails, and creates a single point to apply transformations like compression and caching, improving reliability and spend control.

### LinkedIn Post
Stop wiring apps straight to model APIs.

If you skip your own LLM proxy, you’re flying blind on usage, policy, and spend. The calls look simple in app code—until costs spike, prompts drift, and flaky retries get buried across services.

Here’s the shift: treat LLM access like any other shared infrastructure and interpose a thin gateway you own.

Why it matters:
- Observability: you can’t manage what you can’t see. Central telemetry (who called what, with which model, at what token cost) is the difference between “we think” and “we know.”
- Policy: one place to enforce guardrails instead of trusting every caller to behave.
- Cost control: a single control point to cut waste before it hits the meter.

Technical insight:
- The proxy is where cross‑cutting wins live. Compression and caching don’t belong in every service; they belong at the gateway.
- Patterns like compressing structured payloads (tool outputs, logs, JSON) before they reach the LLM have been reported to reduce tokens in practice; the headroom project highlights this idea. Even a modest reduction adds up when every call flows through one chokepoint.
- Standardize retries/timeouts at the proxy. Remove per‑service guesswork, tame noisy failures, and make backoff behavior visible.
- Log metadata, not secrets. You want enough context to debug and budget, without turning your proxy into a prompt graveyard.

Looking ahead: as more teams embed LLMs, this gateway can become the AI control plane—where observability, policy, and cost guardrails evolve without touching app code.

If you run an internal LLM proxy today, what rule or transform paid off first? If you don’t, what’s stopping you—latency worries, integration cost, or org buy‑in?

### Alternative Hooks
- Calling LLMs directly from app code is the new “write SQL from the UI.” It works—until it doesn’t.
- If you don’t own the call path to your LLM, you don’t own the cost, the policy, or the outage.
- The fastest way to cut LLM waste isn’t a new model. It’s a thin proxy.
