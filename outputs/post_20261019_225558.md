### Topic
Make the LLM gateway your first platform primitive: adopt early for observability, policy, and cost control

### Research Summary
- Core problem: Teams that call LLMs directly via vendor SDKs lose a universal vantage point for telemetry, policy enforcement, and consistent request/response hygiene.
- Key insight: An in-house LLM proxy/gateway centralizes visibility and governance. Adopting it early avoids the high friction of retrofitting controls across many services later.
- Practical payoff: A gateway can apply cost-saving transforms (e.g., compressing logs and RAG chunks) before tokens hit your bill, as demonstrated by proxy-layer token reduction examples.

### LinkedIn Post
Shipping LLM features by calling vendor SDKs from every service feels fast—until you need to answer a basic question: what actually happened?

When each app talks to a model directly, you lose the one place where reality is visible and enforceable. No shared telemetry. No universal policy point. No consistent request/response hygiene. And you can’t try cost levers that only work in the middle.

Platform move: make an in-house LLM proxy your first primitive.

Why it matters:
- Centralize usage telemetry across services instead of piecing it together later.
- Enforce org-wide policies once, instead of rewriting them N times.
- Unlock gateway-level transforms (e.g., compressing logs and RAG chunks) that reduce tokens before they ever become your bill.

The hidden tradeoff here is speed vs. visibility. Direct SDK calls get features out the door, but they create visibility debt. Once five services are in production, retrofitting a gateway is high-friction. You’re asking teams to change code paths just to get back what you should have had on day one: observability, control, and cost levers.

Keep the proxy boring and focused:
- One endpoint your apps call.
- Standardized telemetry on every request/response.
- A few hygiene transforms you can evolve (normalization, compression), and a clear place to enforce policies.

This isn’t about chasing buzzwords. It’s about giving platform engineering a reliable vantage point to observe, enforce, and optimize as LLM usage grows.

Are you running a gateway today? What’s the first transform or policy you’d require on day one?
