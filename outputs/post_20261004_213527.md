### Topic
Kubernetes v1.37: PVC “Unused” condition for safer orphaned volume cleanup

### Research Summary
- Kubernetes v1.37 adds a native Unused condition to PersistentVolumeClaims, managed by the PVC protection controller.
- The condition indicates whether any non-terminated pod currently references a PVC and records when it became idle.
- This Beta feature is enabled by default in v1.37, reducing the need for custom scripts to cross-reference Pods/PVs/PVCs for safe reclamation.
- Practical caveat: pending pods still count as usage, so Unused will not flip while a PVC is referenced by a pending pod.

Source: “Kubernetes v1.37: Tracking When a PersistentVolumeClaim Was Last Used (Beta)”

### LinkedIn Post
Still writing scripts to find zombie PVCs? Kubernetes 1.37 finally gives you a native signal.

PVCs now have an Unused condition (managed by the PVC protection controller) that tells you if any non-terminated pod still references the claim—and records when it became idle via lastTransitionTime. It’s Beta and enabled by default.

Why this matters: no more brittle scripts cross-referencing Pods/PVs/PVCs to guess what’s safe to delete. You get a trusted, in-cluster truth you can automate against, which means cleaner clusters and less storage drift.

Here’s the practical shift:
- Treat Unused=True as “eligible,” not “delete now.”
- Use lastTransitionTime to set an age threshold that matches your risk tolerance.
- Drive a policy loop: mark ➝ notify ➝ reclaim (or archive), instead of ad-hoc cleanup.

Important edge case: pending pods still count as usage. If a pod is stuck but references the PVC, Unused will not flip. Your policy should account for that so you don’t wait forever (or, worse, delete too early).

The real win is cultural: storage cleanup moves from scripts and tribal knowledge to a clear, auditable policy. If your clusters are on v1.37+, this is the simplest, safest path to automated reclamation.
