# GPU workloads break your Kubernetes autoscaling (and how to fix it on GKE)

### Topic

Why GPU workloads break your Kubernetes autoscaling (and how to fix it on GKE)

### Research Summary

- For GPU services, 70–90% of “scaling latency” occurs before the first request: node/GPU capacity spin-up, driver/device plugin init, heavy image/model pulls, PVC readiness, and partial scheduling.
- Vanilla HPA (CPU/memory) reacts after load arrives. For bursty inference, backlog and SLO lead time are better signals; scale from queues, not CPU.
- Common failure modes: bin-packing fragmentation, queue pileups, slow model weight fetch, CSI attach limits, and paid-but-idle GPUs from overprovisioning.
- Practical GKE fixes: KEDA-based backlog autoscaling (Pub/Sub, Cloud Monitoring), small warm GPU pools, pre-pull images/weights, node pools per GPU shape, gang/queueing for batch (Kueue/Volcano), and storage-aware scheduling. Add cost guards and max bounds.

### LinkedIn Post

Your GPU service didn’t “fail to scale” because you lacked replicas. It failed because time-to-readiness dwarfs your autoscaler.

With GPUs, 70–90% of “scaling latency” happens before the first request hits the container: node spin-up, NVIDIA drivers/device plugins, heavy images and model weights, PVCs/CSI limits, and partial scheduling. By the time an HPA on CPU/memory reacts, the spike is gone.

Why it matters: you either pay for idle GPUs “just in case,” or you miss bursts due to cold starts and bin-packing fragmentation.

Make autoscaling work for GPUs on GKE with this minimal playbook:

- Scale on backlog/SLO, not CPU. Use queue depth or request wait time (e.g., Pub/Sub depth, in-queue latency). Optimize for “lead time” vs your P95 SLO.
- Use KEDA for external metrics and scale-to-zero. Drive Deployments/Jobs from Pub/Sub or Cloud Monitoring metrics; cap maxReplicaCount to control spend.
- Keep a warm GPU pool. Run a small min-size GPU node pool (per GPU shape) or use Compute Engine reservations so drivers/plugins are already up.
- Pre-pull heavy images and weights. DaemonSet to crictl pull your images; initContainer to stage model weights to emptyDir or a PVC to avoid repeated GCS/registry hits.
- Prevent partial allocation. Align requests to GPU/MIG shapes; keep fractional GPU mixes on separate node pools. For batch, use a queueing/gang layer (Kueue or Volcano) to avoid “half-scheduled” deadlocks.
- Respect storage and device limits. Use the GCE PD CSI driver (default on GKE), volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer, and watch attach caps so the scheduler and autoscaler get accurate signals.
- Add cost guards. Set max replicas (HPA/KEDA), cluster autoscaler limits, and budget alerts. Track “time-to-first-token” and GPU utilization vs warm capacity.

Reference sketch for GKE: dedicated node pools per GPU type, KEDA ScaledObjects on Pub/Sub (bursty inference) and Job queues (batch), a small warm pool, and a pre-pull DaemonSet. Simple—and it works.

### Alternative Hooks

- Your HPA isn’t slow—your GPUs are cold. That’s why autoscaling “fails” for AI services.
- GPU autoscaling doesn’t break at replica count; it breaks at readiness time. Fix the clock, not the count.
- If you scale GPUs on CPU/memory, you’re measuring the wrong thing. Backlog and SLO lead time win every time.
